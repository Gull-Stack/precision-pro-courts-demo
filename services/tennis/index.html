Tennis Court Excellence in Utah

Tennis remains one of the world's most popular and enduring sports, combining athletic skill, strategy, and mental toughness. Whether you're building a private court for family enjoyment or constructing a multi-court facility for competitive play, the foundation of great tennis starts with a professionally built court.

Utah's climate presents unique challenges and opportunities for tennis court construction. Our experience building courts across the Wasatch Front, from the hot summers of St. George to the temperature variations of Park City, ensures your tennis court will perform consistently year-round.

Tennis Court Types & Surfaces

Hard Court Surfaces

The most popular choice for residential and club facilities. Hard courts offer consistent ball bounce, require less maintenance than other surfaces, and can be customized with color combinations and cushioning systems.

  • Acrylic surface systems
  • Cushioned layer options
  • Multiple color combinations
  • All-weather durability

Clay Court Alternatives

While traditional clay courts require intensive maintenance, modern clay-like surfaces provide similar playing characteristics with reduced upkeep. These surfaces offer slower ball speeds and higher bounces.

  • Har-Tru green clay systems
  • Synthetic clay alternatives
  • Natural red clay options
  • Specialized drainage systems

Grass Court Alternatives

Synthetic turf systems designed for tennis provide the fast, low-bounce characteristics of grass courts without the extensive maintenance requirements of natural grass surfaces.

  • Professional-grade synthetic turf
  • Specialized infill systems
  • Consistent ball behavior
  • Minimal maintenance needs

USTA Regulation Specifications

Every tennis court we construct meets United States Tennis Association standards for dimensions, surface characteristics, and safety requirements.

Official Court Dimensions

  • Playing area: 78 feet × 36 feet
  • Total recommended space: 120 feet × 60 feet
  • Net height: 3 feet 6 inches at posts, 3 feet at center
  • Service boxes: 21 feet × 13.5 feet each
  • Baseline to service line: 18 feet
  • Singles court width: 27 feet (4.5-foot alleys on each side)

Surface Standards

  • ITF-approved surface systems
  • Consistent ball bounce characteristics
  • Appropriate friction coefficients
  • Proper drainage specifications
  • Color contrast for visibility

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does tennis court construction take?

Typical residential tennis court construction takes 7-10 business days after excavation and base preparation. This includes surface installation, line marking, net installation, and final cleanup. Commercial projects may take 2-3 weeks depending on complexity.

What's the difference between cushioned and non-cushioned surfaces?

Cushioned surfaces include shock-absorbing layers beneath the playing surface, reducing stress on players' joints and providing a more comfortable playing experience. Non-cushioned hard courts offer faster play and lower initial cost but may be less comfortable for extended play sessions.

Can I add pickleball lines to my tennis court?

Absolutely! Tennis courts can accommodate multiple pickleball courts. We can add pickleball lines in contrasting colors while maintaining the tennis lines, creating a versatile multi-sport facility that maximizes your court's utility.

How often does a tennis court need resurfacing?

With proper maintenance, tennis courts typically need resurfacing every 4-6 years. Factors like usage intensity, climate exposure, and maintenance quality affect the timeline. Regular cleaning and minor repairs can extend surface life significantly.

What permits are required for tennis court construction?

Most municipalities require building permits for tennis court construction. We handle the permit application process and provide professional drawings and specifications. HOA approval may also be required in some communities.
