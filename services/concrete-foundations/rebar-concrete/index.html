Proven Technology

Rebar-Reinforced Concrete

Rebar-reinforced concrete has long been a trusted method for court foundations. By embedding steel bars (rebar) in the concrete slab, it provides added structural strength and has been used successfully in countless residential court projects. When built on stable soil, rebar slabs offer a solid foundation for pickleball, basketball, or tennis courts.

Key Benefits:

  • Steel reinforcement adds structural support
  • A widely used method in residential court building
  • Straightforward construction with well-known materials

Why Consider Post-Tension?

While rebar works well, sport courts are large, continuous slabs that need to maintain perfect flatness over time. Post-tension concrete is engineered to handle those demands, minimizing movement and maximizing the court's longevity. For many court projects, post-tension is the preferred solution for top-tier performance.

Technical Details

Rebar Foundation Specifications

We follow industry best practices and exceed minimum standards for all rebar installations.

Concrete Strength

Minimum 3,500 PSI compressive strength with appropriate air entrainment for freeze-thaw protection.

Rebar Grade & Size

Grade 60 steel rebar, typically #4 bars (1/2") spaced 18" on center in both directions.

Concrete Thickness

Minimum 4" thickness for residential courts, 5-6" for high-traffic or commercial applications.

Base Requirements

4-6" compacted gravel base with proper drainage and 6-mil vapor barrier below concrete.

Expansion Joints

Strategic placement of expansion joints to control cracking and allow for thermal movement.

Reinforcement Overlap

Minimum 12" lap joints where rebar sections meet, with proper tie-wire connections.

Right Choice When

Is Rebar Right for Your Project?

Rebar concrete is the perfect choice for many court projects, but understanding when it's the right fit helps ensure long-term success.

Ideal Conditions:

  • Stable soil conditions with minimal expansion/contraction
  • Proper drainage around the court area
  • Residential or moderate-use commercial courts
  • Projects with standard timelines and budgets
  • Areas with mild to moderate freeze-thaw cycles

Consider Post-Tension When:

  • Expansive clay soils are present
  • Large court areas (tennis court or larger)
  • Heavy use commercial installations
  • Extreme weather conditions
  • Premium crack-resistance is critical
