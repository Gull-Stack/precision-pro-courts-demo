When to Resurface Your Court

Court resurfacing is a cost-effective way to restore playing surfaces that have deteriorated due to weather, age, or heavy use. Rather than complete replacement, professional resurfacing can add years of life to your court at a fraction of the cost of new construction.

Utah's diverse climate conditions — from freeze-thaw cycles in mountain areas to intense UV exposure in desert regions — can take a toll on court surfaces. Recognizing the signs that indicate resurfacing needs can help you maintain optimal playing conditions and prevent more extensive damage.

Signs Your Court Needs Resurfacing

Surface Cracking

Cracks are the most obvious sign that resurfacing is needed. Small hairline cracks can be repaired, but extensive cracking indicates the need for complete surface renewal.

  • Hairline surface cracks
  • Structural foundation cracks
  • Expansion joint separation
  • Settlement-related cracking

Color Fading & Wear

UV exposure and weather conditions cause court colors to fade and surfaces to become worn, affecting both appearance and playing characteristics.

  • Significant color fading
  • Uneven surface wear patterns
  • Line marking deterioration
  • Surface discoloration

Rough or Slippery Texture

Surface texture changes can affect ball bounce, player traction, and safety. Both overly rough and excessively smooth surfaces indicate resurfacing needs.

  • Loss of surface texture
  • Excessive surface roughness
  • Inconsistent ball bounce
  • Reduced player traction

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does resurfacing last compared to new construction?

Professional resurfacing typically lasts 5-8 years with proper maintenance, providing excellent value compared to new construction. The lifespan depends on usage, maintenance, and climate conditions, but resurfacing can extend total court life significantly.

Can you change court colors during resurfacing?

Absolutely! Resurfacing is the perfect time to update your court's color scheme. We can completely change colors, add multi-sport line systems, or incorporate custom designs and logos into the new surface.

How much does resurfacing cost compared to new court construction?

Resurfacing typically costs 30-50% less than new construction, making it an excellent value for courts with sound structural foundations. The exact savings depend on the extent of repairs needed and surface system selected.

Can you resurface in winter?

Utah winters present challenges for resurfacing due to temperature and moisture conditions. We typically recommend spring through fall for optimal results, though mild winter days may allow for certain types of repair work.

How soon can I play on the resurfaced court?

Most acrylic systems require 48-72 hours of cure time before light foot traffic, with full play recommended after 5-7 days. Weather conditions affect curing time, and we provide specific recommendations for each project.
